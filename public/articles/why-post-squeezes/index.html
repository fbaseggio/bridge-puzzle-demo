Why Post-Squeezes?

A squeeze can wear many disguises. Winners may be in different suits or different hands; entries may require a different sequence of plays. Looking at the position after those winners have been played can reveal what the endings have in common.

Here the common destination is Wwc > a, b: a shared threat with two winners in one hand, and a threat against each defender in the other. Both defenders owe a discard. The post-squeeze notation records that pressure without first choosing which guards they release.

These 13 groups approach that arrangement in different ways. Each begins with a representative, followed by its recorded clones. Ten of the clones retain an extra common-suit winner and reach Wwwc > a, b, the extended post-squeeze.

For comparison, the common threat is always in North’s spades. The leader is shown in every diagram. Open any position in Movie to play the exact cards.
